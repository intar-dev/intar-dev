# intar.dev design context

## Users and jobs

intar.dev serves DevOps learners and working engineers who need to practice diagnosing and repairing real systems. Instructors organize teams, assignments, and progress; operators keep hosts, builds, scenarios, and access healthy. The primary journey is starting or resuming a lab and moving from briefing to repair to verified resolution.

## Voice and tone

Approachable confidence becomes focused repair work and then calm operational control. Copy is direct, specific, and action-led. Learning surfaces explain unfamiliar concepts progressively; operational surfaces assume expertise and stay concise. Errors say what happened, why it matters, and the next recovery action.

## Aesthetic direction

The interface is a calm control room: a field manual meeting a precise instrument. Cool graphite (dark) and soft paper (light) neutrals carry most of the interface and share one ink. Oxide orange marks the next useful action, the focus ring, and the live state. The app sits in an inset panel beside the navigation. The terminal mark remains recognizable. Public and learner surfaces are comfortably paced; run and admin surfaces are compact without becoming cramped. Light and dark themes are equal products, not inversions.

## Principles

1. Make the next repair obvious. The primary action and current system state should remain easy to find.
2. Reveal complexity progressively. Lead with objectives and decisions; move IDs, infrastructure detail, and secondary filters into structured disclosure.
3. Distinguish learning from operations through density. Learner prose breathes; tables, logs, and control surfaces align tightly to a 4px grid.
4. Use status color and motion only when meaningful. Every color has a label or icon companion, and motion communicates transition rather than decoration: reveals ease in, a check that turns verified pops once, and only the one live state in a view breathes.

## Type and spacing

Geist is the display, title, body, control, form, table, and learner-prose face; hierarchy comes from size, weight, and tracking, not from a second family. Geist Mono is reserved for IDs, commands, logs, timers, and terminal-adjacent content. Headings use weights 500 to 600 with tight tracking; nothing heavier. Public display type may be fluid; application typography is fixed in rem. Prose is capped at 68ch and operational numbers use tabular figures. Spacing follows 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, and 96px.

## Avoid

- Generic SaaS card grids and unnecessary nested cards.
- Pill-shaped decoration or large metadata-pill clusters.
- Gradient text; the brand gradient belongs only inside the terminal mark.
- Decorative monospace or code styling on ordinary interface copy.
- Neon, glow, glass, blur, or cyberpunk effects.
- Casual or cartoonish letterforms and heavy display weights.
- Equal density across learning and operations.
- Status conveyed by color alone or decorative motion without meaning.
